# Limited Edition Alpha (`lea`) — standoffs

Genuinely uncertain calls, logged for later discussion rather than decided
unilaterally. Each entry: card name, what's uncertain, what was tagged in
the meantime (a best guess, never left untagged/blocking).

## Word of Command

> Word of Command — Look at target opponent's hand and choose a card from it.
> That player casts that card this turn if able. If that player can't, you
> control that player during the process of casting that spell (as it is
> cast, choose its targets, and so on). Any mana that player spends this way
> can't cause abilities to trigger.

Effectively a one-shot forced-cast / player-control effect with no clean home
in any current theme — not Commandeer (that's changing a PERMANENT's
controller, not a player's decisions during casting a spell), not Discard,
not really Removal. Tagged `atypical` on the closest match for now; open
question whether this is a genuine one-off or the first instance of a
pattern worth a dedicated theme once more "control a player's actions"
effects show up in later sets.

## Camouflage

> Camouflage — Any number of untapped creatures you control can block target
> attacking creature this turn as though it had no blocking restrictions.
> Order those creatures as blockers randomly, then reveal them, and turn the
> rest of your face-down creatures face up. If a creature can't legally
> block the attacking creature, it's removed from combat.

A random-pile blocker-assignment replacement effect. Tagged `atypical` on
Forced Block as the closest theme, but it's really its own thing.

## Illusionary Mask

Lets you cast a creature card face down as a vanilla 2/2, revealed later —
an Alpha-era precursor to modern Morph/Disguise, but no morph/face-down-
casting theme exists yet. Tagged `atypical` on Creature. Worth a dedicated
theme (`morph`?) once real Morph-family cards show up in a later set (Onslaught
onward) and this can be reconciled together.

## Island Sanctuary

> You can't be attacked except by creatures with flying and/or islandwalk,
> unless you didn't draw a card this turn.

A unique defensive-evasion mechanic — tagged `atypical` on Damage Prevention
as the closest fit, but doesn't cleanly match anything.

## Lich

Its loss conditions ("you lose the game" if you can't pay its upkeep
sacrifice cost, or if it leaves the battlefield) are the inverse of
Alternate Win Condition — a way for the CASTER to lose, not a way to make an
opponent lose. Tagged `atypical` on Alt-Win; no theme currently covers
self-loss clauses. Worth watching for a recurring "this card can make YOU
lose" pattern (there are a few of these across Magic's history) before
deciding whether it deserves its own theme.

## Simulacrum

Converts damage dealt to you this turn into life gain, then deals that same
amount as NEW damage to a creature you control. Not Removal (own creature),
not Damage Redirection (a new damage event, not the same damage moved), not
Life Loss (it's damage, not life-loss wording). Tagged Lifegain (for the
gain) plus `atypical` Damage Redirection for the self-inflicted-damage half.

## Stone Giant

Activated ability grants flying to your own creature, then destroys that
same creature at end of turn as a built-in drawback. Not Removal (targets
your own creature), not literal Sacrifice (a destroy effect, not
sacrifice-cost wording). Tagged `atypical` Sacrifice as the closest
conceptual fit for the self-destroy-for-value shape.

## Two-Headed Giant of Foriys

"Can block an additional creature each combat" (multi-blocking) has no
clean home — not Menace (that's about needing 2+ blockers on the attacker's
side), not Forced Block. Left untagged for that clause.

## Manabarbs

Reacts to the act of tapping a land for mana (punishing it with damage)
rather than disabling/producing/granting mana itself — doesn't cleanly fit
Mana's produce/consume/grant definitions. Tagged `atypical` on Mana
alongside a clean Face Damage tag for the actual damage effect. Only one
example so far; not promoting to a theme yet.
